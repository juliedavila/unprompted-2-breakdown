This page is a secondary source. It reports what speakers said and showed at [un]prompted 2, as recorded in the conference's public playlist, and it has not been reviewed by the speakers or the organizers. Watch the talk before relying on a record; every claim links to its moment in the video.

How it was made

  • Source. The playlist lists 92 recordings. Three are unavailable (positions 31, 44 and 69), which leaves 89.
  • Transcripts are YouTube's automatic English captions, cleaned and time-marked. Automatic captions garble names, products and numbers.
  • Slides are frames taken from the video where the picture changed. Slides are the authority for spellings and figures: where a slide and the captions disagree, the record uses the slide and says so under its caveats.
  • Each record was written by an AI model (Anthropic's Claude) that read one talk's full transcript and every captured slide, working to a fixed schema. A script checked each record for completeness, and two further model passes revised the wording for fairness and clarity.
  • No person reviewed each record before publication. A person directed the work and its reviews. Expect errors in individual records, and treat each one as a pointer to the recording.
  • The cross-talk reading at the top was drafted with Claude and edited after an independent review. The figures it relies on were checked a second time, by the same AI system, against the transcript or the slide.
  • The notes headed "What published research says" come from a search of the academic literature with Elicit, one query per finding. The papers were read at the level of their abstracts, many are preprints that have not been peer reviewed, and the notes are a starting point for reading and no survey. Each paper is linked.
  • No talk's claims were checked against outside sources. A number about a talk is the speaker's number.
  • Evidence grades describe what the talk showed for a claim: measured (the speaker's own data or experiment), demo (shown working, live or recorded), case (a specific real incident or deployment), anecdote (a story without checkable detail), or assertion (stated without support in the talk). A grade is a statement about the talk, and a 25-minute slot is short: an assertion on stage may be well supported in a paper the speaker did not have time to show.
  • Signal scores and assessments are judgments about the evidence presented in the recording. They run generous: {{TOP}} of {{SESSIONS}} sessions scored 4 or 5. They say nothing about the speaker or the work beyond the talk.

Gaps

  • Two talks have no record. Both are exploit-development talks. The AI system declined to write up their detail, which reflects the model's limits and says nothing about the talks.
    • Ryan Austin and Thai Duong, "PHPocalypse: Exploiting php2shell" (position 34).
    • Brendan Dolan-Gavitt, "A Tale of Two Chains" (position 60). From the transcript: a first Chrome full-chain attempt took two months; a second took six days and about $40,000 in API spend, against a full-chain bonus the speaker put at $250,000; the run used up to 64 sub-agents with a running ledger.
  • Four talks are covered at summary level, with findings described as a public advisory would and exploit mechanics left out: Hacking Lean Proofs (07), Every Dog Has its 0-day (32), 0-Click Remote Exploits in an AI World (58) and Prompt, Patch, Prove (64).
  • Some slides were missed. A frame is captured only when the picture changes enough, so a slide shown briefly can be absent. Records note a missing slide where the transcript made it evident.
