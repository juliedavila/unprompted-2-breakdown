This page is a secondary source. It reports what speakers said and showed at [un]prompted 2, as recorded in the conference's public playlist, and it has not been reviewed by the speakers or the organizers. Watch the talk before relying on a record; every claim links to its moment in the video.

How it was made

  • Source. The playlist lists 92 recordings. Three are unavailable (positions 31, 44 and 69), which leaves 89.
  • Transcripts are YouTube's automatic English captions, cleaned and time-marked. Automatic captions garble names, products and numbers.
  • Slides are frames taken from the video where the picture changed. Slides are the authority for spellings and figures: where a slide and the captions disagree, the record uses the slide and says so under its caveats.
  • Each record was written by an AI model (Anthropic's Claude) that read one talk's full transcript and every captured slide, working to a fixed schema. A script checked each record for completeness. The cross-talk reading at the top was also drafted with Claude, and the figures it relies on were checked against the transcript or the slide.
  • Nothing was checked against sources outside the talks. A number here is the speaker's number.
  • Evidence grades describe what the talk showed for a claim: measured (the speaker's own data or experiment), demo, case (a specific real incident or deployment), anecdote, or assertion. A grade is a statement about the talk, and a 25-minute slot is short: an assertion on stage may be well supported in a paper the speaker did not have time to show.
  • Signal scores and assessments are judgments about the evidence presented in the recording. They run generous: {{TOP}} of {{SESSIONS}} sessions scored 4 or 5. They say nothing about the speaker or the work beyond the talk.

Gaps

  • Two talks have no record. Both are exploit-development talks whose detail the AI system would not write up.
    • Ryan Austin and Thai Duong, "PHPocalypse: Exploiting php2shell" (position 34).
    • Brendan Dolan-Gavitt, "A Tale of Two Chains" (position 60). From the transcript: a first Chrome full-chain attempt took two months; a second took six days and about $40,000 in API spend, against a full-chain bonus the speaker put at $250,000; the run used up to 64 sub-agents with a running ledger.
  • Four talks are covered at summary level, with findings described as a public advisory would and exploit mechanics left out: Hacking Lean Proofs (07), Every Dog Has its 0-day (32), 0-Click Remote Exploits in an AI World (58) and Prompt, Patch, Prove (64).
  • Some slides were missed. A frame is captured only when the picture changes enough, so a slide shown briefly can be absent. Records note this where it could be told.
